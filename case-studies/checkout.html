Project Overview

For a luxury fine jewelry e-commerce brand, checkout is the final and most fragile step of a considered, high-value purchase. Every extra field, unclear cost, or unfamiliar payment option is a place hesitation can creep in and a transaction can stall — especially for customers used to a quieter, more curated buying experience.

This project brought together two connected initiatives aimed at that fragility: streamlining checkout itself into a single, transparent step with Express Checkout, and giving customers a native, flexible way to pay through Easy Pay installments — extended from the product page all the way into checkout.

The Challenge

Customers arriving at checkout with full intent to buy were dropping off — some from a multi-step form that interrupted momentum with page loads, others from the friction of having to type in card details when they'd have preferred a saved wallet, and others simply because paying for a high-value item in full, in one charge, wasn't the way they wanted to pay.

Objective / Goal

Reduce checkout abandonment and grow conversion by (1) collapsing checkout into one transparent step with express, one-tap payment options, and (2) making Easy Pay installments available as a genuine payment method at every point a customer might decide to use it — not just on the product page.

Empathize

Research

Data-Driven Insights

Heatmaps, analytics, and customer surveys on the existing checkout surfaced the friction points that shaped the roadmap — where attention dropped off, which fields caused hesitation, and where customers abandoned the flow before reaching payment.

Key Questions

What is causing customers to abandon checkout after reaching the payment step?
Which payment methods would reduce the perceived effort of completing a high-value purchase?
Would offering installment payments change a customer's willingness to check out?

This is what I found

Luxury customers want speed without losing exclusivity. Customers wanted a fast, secure transaction — but not one that felt generic or stripped of the brand's sense of craft and trust.

Manual form-filling was a bigger blocker than price. Customers with a stored PayPal, Amazon Pay, or Apple Pay wallet were more likely to abandon at the address/payment fields than to abandon over cost.

Budget flexibility mattered more than most other friction points. For higher-ticket items, the ability to split payment into installments was a stronger purchase driver than incremental UI polish.

Define

Project Goals

Business Goals

Increase checkout-to-order conversion (C2O), grow revenue per visitor (RPV) and revenue per qualified visitor (RPQV), and reduce reliance on discounting as the primary conversion lever.

User Goals

Check out quickly and securely without re-entering information already saved elsewhere, understand the full cost before committing, and have a payment method that fits their budget — not just their card.

Testing

Usability & A/B Testing

Each change — One-Step Checkout, Express Checkout, and Easy Pay — was validated through a pre/post live comparison rather than a single moderated test, given the transactional, funnel-level nature of checkout.

Metrics Tracked

C2O (Checkout-to-Order): the percentage of checkout sessions that complete an order. RPV / RPQV: revenue per visitor and per qualified visitor. Order volume: average orders per day.

Closing

Future Impact

  • Smarter Express Checkout placement. Investigate the 1% C2O dip — test whether repositioning or resizing the Express row recovers that loss while keeping the order-volume gain.
  • Expand Easy Pay eligibility signals. Surface installment eligibility earlier in the funnel (PLP, cart drawer) so more customers arrive at checkout already aware of the option.
  • Personalize payment method ordering. Use purchase history to reorder Express Checkout and Easy Pay by the method each customer is statistically most likely to convert with.
  • Extend installments to more categories. Explore raising the Easy Pay order-value ceiling for the highest-ticket engagement and bridal pieces.
